Every AI agent app, compared with MausBot

Grok Bot, Muse, dots, Cue, Instinct, Bops, Cowork, OpenClaw and Hermes, then the open-source clones (open-dot, OpenBot, OpenMuse, Rakazo), each against MausBot, with a verdict.

Two kinds of agent app shipped in 2026. The big ones, Grok Bot, Muse, dots, Cue, Instinct, Bops and Claude Cowork on the closed side and OpenClaw and Hermes on the open side, each keep the model, the computer or the safety decision somewhere other than with you. The "open X" clones, open-dot, OpenBot, OpenMuse, OpenInstinct and Rakazo, publish their code but are mostly one agent, one vendor, or a demo. MausBot is the open-source one that is a finished app: a team of bots on any model, on your own machine, free under Apache 2.0. One page per rival below.

Orgo (Organic Intelligence, Inc.)

Bops alternative

Bops, launched by Orgo on October 6, 2026, is a Mac app that runs a team of business-ops bots on OpenAI models, with Orgo cloud computers and, on paid plans, phone numbers and email addresses the bots can own, under a fair-source licence. MausBot is the open source alternative: a team of bots on any model, local-first, free under Apache 2.0, on Mac, Windows and Ubuntu.

MausBot is the open source Hark Pro alternative: a team of bots on any model, on your own machine, Apache 2.0, with a desktop app and phone apps.

The verdict

Put the tables together and one app clears every bar the others each miss one of. The closed products are polished but you rent them: one vendor's model, their computer, your data on their side, and a region list that may not include you. The big open-source agents, OpenClaw and Hermes, give you the code and a gateway to administer, with the safety switch off by default. The clones give you the code but, as of the dates above, each is one agent on one provider, a service to deploy, or a demo. MausBot is the only one in these tables that is all of the following at once: open source under Apache 2.0, a team of bots rather than one assistant, any model per bot on the subscription you already pay for, an approval card before every shell command by default, a desktop app for Mac, Windows and Ubuntu with iOS and Android companions, and a Cloud plan when you want the laptop closed. On the evidence in these pages, MausBot is the best AI agent app you can run today, and the one we would pick even if we had not built it.

Where a rival does something MausBot does not, its page says so in a section called "what it still does better". We keep those honest because the comparison is worth nothing otherwise.

How to choose

Start with one question: do you want one assistant or a team? Muse, dots and Instinct give you one agent and hide the rest; Grok Bot, Cue, Bops and MausBot give you named agents you can put in a room. Then ask which subscription you already pay for, where you live, where your data may live, whether you need to read the code, and whether the agent has to keep working with your laptop closed. Our five-question guide walks through it, the five-way comparison puts the big four beside MausBot in one table, and the open-source round-up does the same for the clones.

Why the open-source one is different

The closed agents differ in price and polish but share three properties: the model is a vendor, the computer is theirs, and you cannot read the code. MausBot inverts all three. The model is a setting on each bot; the computer is the one in front of you, or a cloud desktop when you ask for one; and the approval gate, the harness on 127.0.0.1 , and the folder your data sits in are things a reviewer can open before the first message is sent.
